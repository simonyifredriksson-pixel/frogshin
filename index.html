GAME MODES

  • Everyone votes between rounds. Most votes wins; with 3+ players you also pick how many taggers there are (up to one fewer than the player count).
  • Tag. Taggers have unlimited kunai. Hit a runner and you swap — they become it, you go free. Brief immunity stops instant tag-backs.
  • Infection. Same throw, but the runner joins the taggers. Ends when everyone is infected, or the survivors run out the clock.
  • Classic FFA. Normal deathmatch — katana, kunai, damage and respawns. Most kills wins.
  • Team Battle. Two sides, 1v1 up to 5v5, no friendly fire.
  • Juggernaut. One player becomes an armoured toad with a huge katana, endless kunai and a mountain of health — but moves at half speed and barely benefits from sprinting. Its katana hits for a flat 50. It cannot grapple — G instead charges a leap at whatever you are looking at, winding up for 0.5s to 1.5s depending how far you are throwing yourself. It wins by putting every frog out; the frogs win by bringing it down. Needs 3 players.
  • In Tag and Infection there is no damage at all — both the kunai and the katana tag instead of wounding, so it is a pure chase.
  • Spectating. Knocked out of a juggernaut round, you keep flying around with endless kunai but are invisible and cannot touch anyone (or be touched), and cannot take crates. You rejoin when the round ends.
  • The Croaklands. The main game. Solo, and it saves itself.
    Why you are walking. You wake up in Mirefoot, which was your village until eleven days ago. Frogath's soldiers took everybody who could walk and burned the rest. The next roof you can see is Croakhollow; go and find somebody who saw it happen, and then follow the column north.
    How the country is shut. Nothing tells you a region is locked, because none of them are. There is a thing standing in every road: a barred gate with something in it, a ford nothing crosses, a bridge its builder has never let anybody use. Put that thing down and the road opens — and the village behind it takes the black banners down, the soldiers leave the square, the boards come off the windows and the people say something different. Thirty-seven guardians, ten optional ones nobody mentions, and Frogath at the end.
    Kunai are finite. You leave home with twenty and they do not come back. Every blade after those twenty is one you found — in a chest, on a body, in a ruin, or bought from a market stall for froglets. Guardians drop a good handful, so a fight you had to learn pays for itself.
    Everything opens for real. Chests have lids that swing up and the thing inside comes out of them. Levers throw and lift the portcullis they are wired to. Doors swing. Swords come off their stands. There are twenty-eight carved stones and half-burned books scattered across the country and between them they explain what Frogath is actually doing up there — read them in any order, or none.
    You gain levels, hearts, attack and defence; you find weapons, armour, food and materials and carry them in a bag you open with TAB. You are the only ninja in the country — everybody else is a farmer, a fishmonger, a smith, a scholar or a child. Die and you wake up at the last village or shrine you stood in, keeping everything. Walk away from a guardian and the fight resets — so a fight you cannot win yet is a fight to come back to.
  • The Dungeon. Solo. Fifteen boss rooms, each guardian tougher than the last, and endless kunai throughout. Choose checkpoints (die and restart the room) or no checkpoints (die and go back to room one). Clearing a room heals you. Every attack in the dungeon shows a marker on the floor before it can hurt you, and the marker is exactly the size of the thing that hits you. A red ring means move out of it. An amber ring is a ground wave — jump it (a dash works too). Read it, dodge it, punish the recovery. At the bottom is FROGATH, THE FIRST CROAK. He floats, he does not miss, and he is the reason the other fourteen were put there.
  • How a guardian fights, and why spamming does not work. A guardian on its feet watching you guards: a thrown kunai lands at a fraction of its damage and you will see it turned aside. It cannot guard while it is committing to an attack, and it cannot guard its recovery — the beat after an attack, which takes double from a blade thrown from close in. A hit on the head ignores the guard completely. Cut it with the katana to break its poise and it reels, wide open. And if you stand at range throwing, it stops tolerating the distance and comes to you.
    The loop is: read the ring, get out of it, close, cut, break its poise, spend one blade on the opening, and back out. Some of them raise a shell that thrown blades do nothing to at all — walk in and cut it off, because breaking one staggers them. Wide attacks sweep kunai out of the air.
    Every one of them changes shape two to four times as its health falls, and each change unlocks two new attacks and makes the floor of the arena dangerous. Everything is still telegraphed, every wind-up is still readable, and every sequence still has a way out of it.

NINJA TECHNIQUE

  • Wall jump. Hug any wall in mid-air and press SPACE to kick off it.
  • Swing, don't winch. The tongue is a real rope — build speed by swinging wide, then release with SPACE for a launch.
  • Chain everything. Landing a grapple refills your air dash and flip. Dash → grapple → flip → dash crosses the whole map.
  • Lanterns are targets. The glowing lanterns are always grappleable and the crosshair opens up when one is in reach.
  • The third swing hits hardest. Land all three combo hits — the finisher does heavy damage and big knockback.
  • Dash through attacks. The dash has brief invulnerability frames.
  • Kunai hit for 25 — or 50 to the head. Press 2 to equip them and LMB to throw. They fly dead straight and very fast, so the crosshair is exactly where they land. In the arena you start with 10 and the glowing crates hold 5 more each, replaced every 30 seconds. In the Croaklands you start with twenty and there are no crates — see the mode notes.
  • Practise on the dummies. The straw dummies on the arena platforms show exactly how much damage each hit does.
  • Watch your stamina. Sprinting drains it and every jump costs a chunk. Empty it and you cannot sprint or jump until it climbs back to 70% — so keep some in reserve for the escape. Dashing and grappling are free.
